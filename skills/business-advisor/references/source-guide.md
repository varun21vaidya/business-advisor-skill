# Source guide

Use these sources as durable starting points. Recheck current versions and local applicability whenever the advice depends on current facts or law. This list is selective; it is not a claim to exhaustive internet coverage.

## Market research and competitive analysis

- U.S. Small Business Administration, [Market research and competitive analysis](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/plan-your-business/market-research-competitive-analysis). Practical distinction between customer research and competitor analysis, including direct and indirect competitors. U.S.-oriented resource; adapt methods, not legal assumptions, for other countries.
- Y Combinator Startup Library, [Do Things That Don’t Scale](https://www.ycombinator.com/library/96-do-things-that-don-t-scale). Useful for early manual customer acquisition, onboarding, and learning close to users. Do not treat it as a universal mandate to ignore cost or scalability indefinitely.

## Marketing truthfulness and persuasion

- U.S. Federal Trade Commission, [Advertising and marketing basics](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/advertising-marketing). U.S. guidance on truthful, non-deceptive, evidence-backed claims and endorsements. For any jurisdiction, verify the applicable requirements with the relevant local regulator and legislation.
- U.S. Federal Trade Commission, [Advertising FAQs: A guide for small business](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/advertising-faqs-guide-small-business). Useful reminder that implied claims and omissions matter, not only literal wording.

## Finance and cash flow

- SCORE, [Finance Basics](https://www.score.org/sites/default/files/2023-01/SBE%20Finance%20Basics%20vDC8-202212%20vFinal.pdf). Introductory small-business finance and cash-flow material. Use as a learning resource, not as personalized accounting, tax, or investment advice.
- For country-specific tax, accounting, payments, consumer, employment, and data rules, prefer the relevant official government or regulator source and a qualified local professional where needed.

## Applying sources responsibly

- Prefer primary sources for law, regulations, platform terms, and current company policies. Prefer original research or transparent datasets for market-size and performance claims.
- Record publication/update date and jurisdiction. Search snippets are leads, not confirmation; open the source and verify the relevant passage.
- Do not use a general startup essay as proof a specific business will work. Convert advice into a testable hypothesis and measure it in the user's context.
- If credible sources conflict, explain the disagreement, differences in scope, and what evidence is missing.
