# Business Advisor operating playbook

## Strategy and business models

- Write the customer segment, purchase occasion, alternative, reason to switch, route to reach them, payer and cost to serve in one paragraph. Make an explicit choice about which segment or channel gets the next investment. Use the [Business Model Canvas](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/the-business-model-canvas) when interdependencies are unclear and [Testing Business Ideas](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/testing-business-ideas-book) to convert assumptions into tests.
- Estimate reachable demand from observed channel capacity and conversion. Use market size for context. For each option, compare incremental contribution, cash required, management time, time to evidence and downside. Use past projects to challenge optimistic cost and duration estimates. [HM Treasury appraisal guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/green-book-supplementary-guidance-optimism-bias)
- For a marketplace, measure match or purchase rate by niche, time to first sale, buyer and seller repeat rates, seller concentration and platform-generated orders. Separate buyers acquired by the platform from sellers' existing customers. Ask buyers how they found the specific seller, then compare with referral data and preserve an unknown bucket. [Marketplace metrics](https://a16z.com/13-metrics-for-marketplace-companies/)
- If sellers fulfill orders themselves, compare platform checkout with the direct channel on total price, time, buyer protection and remedy when something goes wrong. Interview buyers who leave checkout and sellers who receive direct inquiries. Evaluate protection, consolidated tracking, or platform-generated discovery as hypotheses. Study both sides' incentives before adding friction. [Trust and disintermediation research](https://questromworld.bu.edu/platformstrategy/wp-content/uploads/sites/49/2019/07/PlatStrat2019_paper_38.pdf)
- For an introductory offer already promised to customers or partners, preserve the promised term and define its start, eligibility, covered fees and any separate charges. Estimate subsidy per order and cap pilot exposure through cohort size, funded operating capacity and enrollment limits. Validate willingness to pay with later cohorts or optional services while honoring existing commitments.

Use the business model to choose the diagnostic before recommending growth:

| Model | Diagnostic | Practical decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Professional services | Utilization, billable rate, delivery margin, collections | Raise price, adjust scope, or add capacity |
| Retail or manufacturing | Margin by SKU, inventory turns, cash tied up in stock, defects | Change assortment, purchasing, or production |
| Subscription | Activation, retention and contribution by cohort | Fix onboarding or retention before raising acquisition spend |
| Enterprise sales | Win rate, deal cycle, procurement, account concentration | Requalify the pipeline or change how buyers evaluate the offer |
| Marketplace | Successful matches, repeat transactions, costs and cash on both sides | Focus on a narrower market or change transaction services |
| Nonprofit or membership organization | Program cost, mission outcomes, retention, restricted funding | Reallocate resources or change delivery |

For an established business, find the bottleneck in existing sales, financial or delivery data before proposing a pilot. For a turnaround, prioritize liquidity and continuity. For a new venture, seek behavioral evidence and test how the offer will reach customers.

## Finance, budgeting and cash flow

- Build a rolling 13-week cash forecast when cash is tight. For each week show starting unrestricted cash, dated receipts, dated payments and ending cash. Separate seller proceeds, tax amounts, payment holds and reserves from cash available to operate. Reconcile to bank balances and processor settlements weekly. Set a minimum cash balance and a date to act before breaching it. [FDIC cash flow participant guide](https://www.fdic.gov/consumer-resource-center/mssb-m10-pg.pdf)
- Build a monthly budget with an accountable owner, fixed commitments, variable spend and a spending limit for each experiment. Record actuals and explain deviations through price, volume, timing or a changed assumption. Distinguish P&L, balance sheet and cash movements. Verify principal-versus-agent treatment and local reporting obligations when revenue recognition matters. [IAS 7 cash flow overview](https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/list-of-standards/ias-7-statement-of-cash-flows/)
- Define GMV and the order denominator before calculating take rate. Estimate contribution per completed order using platform revenue less incremental payment, service, support, expected refunds or claims borne by the platform, and subsidies. Show acquisition cost separately and label fully loaded versus marginal figures. Track cash liability under payout and refund timing.
- Illustrative marketplace order in currency units. On 2,000 of GMV at a hypothetical 8% take rate, revenue is 160. If processing costs 40, expected claims cost 30 and support costs 35, contribution is 55 before acquisition and fixed costs. At zero commission and the same costs, contribution is −105. With 22,000 monthly fixed costs and 55 contribution, operating break-even is 400 orders per month before acquisition. A zero or negative contribution has no finite break-even volume under unchanged assumptions. [SBA break-even calculator](https://bec.www.sba.gov/breakevenpointcalculator)
- Stress-test slower payouts, higher damage or return rates, and worse conversion. Use observed cohort contribution for acquisition payback. Avoid treating forecast lifetime value as proven. Compare financing options using timing, repayment, dilution or control and downside; verify current terms with primary sources.

## Sales, persuasion, speaking and copy

- Establish the prospect's current situation, problem, cost of leaving it unresolved, and value of a solution. Qualify buyer authority, timing, budget, and the next mutually agreed action. Review conversion by pipeline stage and record why prospects decline. [Huthwaite SPIN methodology](https://www.huthwaiteinternational.com/spin-methodology)
- Write outreach as a specific reason for contacting the person, a concrete offer, an accurate description of obligations, and one small next action. For a prospective marketplace seller, demonstrate a real storefront and explain who handles checkout, shipping, payouts and support. Ask for a short walkthrough or a small initial catalogue based on what the team can support.
- In negotiation, list each party's interests, objective criteria, likely alternatives, your walk-away terms and trades you can make. Summarize terms and responsibilities in writing. In networking, make a specific request, offer relevant help, ask permission before introducing people and follow through. [Harvard Program on Negotiation](https://www.pon.harvard.edu/daily/negotiation-skills-daily/principled-negotiation-focus-interests-create-value/)
- For speaking, define the audience decision, lead with its situation, show a concrete example or demo, give evidence and end with one request. Rehearse within the allotted time and prepare answers for the hardest objections. For copy, use buyer vocabulary from research, state the offer and conditions plainly and make the next action visible. [Toastmasters speech guidance](https://www.toastmasters.org/resources/public-speaking-tips/How-to-Write-and-Deliver-a-Speech), [GOV.UK user needs](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/plan-manage-content/identify-user-needs/)
- Use buyer psychology to test whether true proof reduces uncertainty, clearer choices reduce effort, or actionable information improves decisions. Show authentic reviews, actual inventory and total price. Treat persuasion frameworks as hypotheses, and check local rules for endorsements and commercial disclosures. [FTC advertising guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/advertising-faqs-guide-small-business), [ASCI](https://www.ascionline.in/)

## Marketing, customer research and analytics

- Choose one segment, promise, creative and distribution channel per early test. Set maximum spend, qualified conversion and guardrails such as cancellations, damage and support time. Compare channels by fulfilled orders and contribution, including staff time where material. Use tags or referral codes where appropriate and report unattributed orders.
- Interview people about their last relevant transaction and ask what they actually did, paid or abandoned. Recruit outside the business's existing network, include lost and inactive users, seek contradictory examples and distinguish anecdotes from observed patterns. Get consent before recording. Validate through paid behavior or a meaningful commitment where appropriate. [GOV.UK interviews](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/using-in-depth-interviews), [YC manual recruitment](https://www.ycombinator.com/library/96-do-things-that-don-t-scale)
- Define events and denominators before analysis. Check for duplicate and missing events, changes in mix, attribution gaps and censoring. For controlled tests, specify assignment unit, primary outcome, minimum useful effect, window and guardrails. Check whether the split matches assignment, especially after filtering. Use descriptive results and uncertainty for small pilots, and consider spillover across sellers and buyers. [NIST statistics handbook](https://www.nist.gov/programs-projects/nistsematech-engineering-statistics-handbook), [Microsoft experiment interpretation research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/2017-08-KDDMetricInterpretationPitfalls.pdf)

## Technical decisions, projects and operations

- Compare manual, purchased and built solutions on coverage, total cost, integration effort, data export, vendor dependence and recovery if the service fails. State the trigger for moving from manual work to automation. For live commerce prioritize correct prices, inventory, payment reconciliation, auditable changes and an operator's recovery path. Test process handoffs from listing to payout, and rehearse damaged, missing and canceled orders.
- Map a project to deliverables, one accountable owner per outcome, dependencies, milestones, acceptance criteria and review dates. Reserve management time for the current bottleneck. Batch routine work and limit concurrent projects. When late, split scope around the outcome and surface resource constraints early.
- In operations, trace where work waits or returns for correction. Measure cycle time, quality failures and exception cost before automating. Maintain clear access ownership, recoverable backups and a response plan proportional to the data and business risk. Track delivery speed and stability over time for the same service. [NIST small business cybersecurity](https://www.nist.gov/itl/smallbusinesscyber/nist-cybersecurity-framework-0), [DORA delivery metrics](https://dora.dev/guides/dora-metrics/)

## Leadership, adaptability and resilience

- Delegate using an outcome, rationale, decision authority, deadline, example of acceptable work and check-in date. Ask the person to explain their approach so assumptions surface early. Give feedback on the observed action and impact, then ask about intent and agree on the next action. [Center for Creative Leadership](https://www.ccl.org/articles/leading-effectively-articles/closing-the-gap-between-intent-vs-impact-sbii/)
- For recurring problems, reproduce and describe the failure, check where it starts, distinguish likely causes from known ones, change one meaningful variable where possible and measure recurrence. For uncertain decisions, record the options, evidence, assumptions, tradeoffs and what would reverse the choice. Review at the agreed time or on material new evidence.
- When under pressure, identify the irreversible commitment, split it into decisions that can be revisited, and schedule a review with someone who can challenge assumptions. Manage workload and recovery time when stress is affecting judgment. Keep any support discussion within the user's goals, with no diagnosis. [WHO stress management guide](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240003927)

## Jurisdiction and industry verification

- Identify the countries or regions where the company operates, sells, collects payments, employs people, or handles data. Map who contracts, invoices, receives money, and handles refunds. Verify applicable rules, amendments, effective dates and provider terms using official sources for each jurisdiction.
- Identify sector-specific requirements as needed. For example, check licensing for regulated services, product safety for physical goods, labor rules for staffing, or privacy and commercial messaging rules for digital channels. Record what applies, what remains uncertain, and which question needs qualified local review.
